01 · The answer

How AI vendors actually charge

No one pays for “tokens.” They pay for access, users, work done, and risk covered. An enterprise quote is a bundle of these parts.

01

Platform fee

Pay to enter. Covers the software, security, and basic support.

Example: the wealth firm pays a flat fee to have its own SARVAX workspace.

02

User fee

Pay per person. Counts advisors, assistants, or operations staff.

Example: 10 advisors and 3 assistants get seats.

03

Work fee

Pay for output. Counts meetings processed, documents created, or tasks completed.

Example: processing 500 meetings costs more than 50.

04

Product fee

Pay for what you use. Adds only the tools you need—meetings, compliance, or portfolio analysis.

Example: start with Meeting Assistant, add Compliance later.

05

Setup fee

One-time charge. Covers migration, custom workflows, and training.

Example: map Zoho CRM fields into SARVAX and test the connection.

06

Control fee

Pay for risk. Covers advanced security, audits, private hosting, or 24/7 support.

Example: single sign-on, seven-year record retention, and priority support.

Real quote example

A 10-advisor firm wants meetings turned into completed work

SARVAX prepares meetings, captures decisions, updates Zoho CRM, and creates compliance tasks. The quote includes: base workspace, 10 advisor seats, Meeting + Compliance products, 500 meetings/month, and one-time CRM setup. No private hosting = no charge. No special retention = no charge.

Final price = only what the client actually uses. These are six possible charges—not six mandatory fees.

Why vendors charge differently: Jump charges per advisor because advisors use the meeting tool. Holistiplan charges per household because each plan serves one household. n8n charges per workflow run because the work varies. The meter follows the value delivered. But watch for credit models that obscure the true cost per meeting.

04 · Pricing mechanics

The charge follows the value delivered

A meeting tool charges per person. A planning tool charges per household. An automation platform charges per task completed. The meter matches the work.

Predictable workload

Seat or firm subscription

Simple to buy and budget when usage is similar across customers.

→

Variable workload

Credits or executions

Tracks the amount of work, but requires transparent definitions and spend controls.

→

Measurable completion

Outcome charge

Aligns payment to a successful result, but only when “success” is objective and auditable.

The trap: Some vendors hide the real cost in “credits” or “executions.” You buy 10,000 credits, but one meeting uses 50 credits, one document uses 100 credits, and one workflow uses 500 credits. You think you bought cheap. You actually bought a meter that can drain fast. Ask: “How many credits does one advisor meeting actually cost?” If they can’t answer in plain English, walk away.

05 · Quote drivers

What makes the price go up

The sticker price is just the start. More people, more work, more integrations, or higher security requirements increase the quote.

More people

More advisors, assistants, or operations staff

Expands platform access and operating surface.

More work

More meetings, documents, tasks, or outcomes

Raises infrastructure and support demand.

More data

Migration, retention, knowledge sources, and reconciliation

Adds implementation work and ongoing storage.

More integration

Standard connection versus custom system work

A connector is not automatically a fee; custom engineering may be.

More control

Identity, permissions, audit, compliance, and private hosting

Raises governance and operational requirements.

More service

Training, implementation, support, and service level

Adds people and response commitments.

06 · Connector decision

Connectors should not be a hidden fee

Standard integrations come with the plan. Custom work—new data mapping, engineering, or testing—may cost extra. But don’t pay just because a connector exists.

Bundle

Standard connection

Existing, supported, repeatable, and low-maintenance.

Scope

Custom integration

New data mapping, engineering, testing, migration, or client-specific workflow.

Scope

Material operating burden

Paid third-party API, dedicated hosting, special retention, or compliance obligation.

07 · Founder decision

Price for value, not for complexity

Research shows what others charge. SARVAX’s price must match what buyers actually pay for—time saved, errors prevented, and capacity created.

Step 1

Pick the meter

Per advisor, per firm, per meeting, per document, or per completed task.

Step 2

Know your cost

Model calls, documents, meetings, tools, retries, storage, support, and compliance logging.

Step 3

Know the value

Time recovered, errors prevented, faster completion, capacity created, and revenue protected.

Step 4

Test with real buyers

Run pilots with clear scope, approval, success metric, and contract conversion point.

What this report approves

A pricing experiment, not a fixed rate card.

  • Keep a simple base for predictable access.
  • Meter only work that varies materially.
  • Scope custom work separately.
  • Keep approvals and spend limits visible.
The bottom line: Buyers don’t care about tokens, models, or architectures. They care about outcomes. Price SARVAX on the work it completes and the value it delivers—not on the complexity it hides.
